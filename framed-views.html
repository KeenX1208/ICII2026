NAU-CHINA × NNU-Nanjing-China

Why We Connect

Both teams are based in Nanjing and chose liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS). NAU-CHINA creates a programmable toolbox to answer "how to regulate condensates", while NNU-Nanjing-China applies membraneless organelles to indigo biosynthesis, answering "how to improve industrial efficiency". One creates the method, the other applies it.

Joint Output

Together they complete the full chain of condensate technology from basic genetic tool design to industrial implementation in sustainable dye production.

What We See in Each Other

NAU-CHINA's View:

"We interpret the boundary philosophy of membraneless organelles through Suzhou gardens—separated yet connected. NNU-Nanjing-China's application proves our toolbox can solve real-world industrial challenges."

NNU-Nanjing-China's View:

"We focus on preserving the Nantong tie-dye cultural heritage. NAU-CHINA's tunable condensation modules provide a broader theoretical foundation for our biosynthetic indigo spatial compartmentalization strategy."

BUCT-China × HUST-China

Why We Connect

Both teams are "turning waste into new materials", but their outputs are entirely different. BUCT turns kitchen waste into transparent, degradable structural material, while HUST turns waste cotton into aesthetic structural color material.

Joint Output

A comparative exploration of biomaterial properties: Vessel vs. Decoration. One pursues functional load-bearing capacity and recycling, while the other pursues sensory color presentation.

What We See in Each Other

BUCT-China's View:

"We rely on host-guest interactions and hydrogen bonds, acting like 'sunmao' (mortise and tenon) for physical strength. HUST's biomimetic approach to replicating kingfisher feathers shows us the artistic potential of biological self-assembly."

HUST-China's View:

"Our Diancui-mimetic material revives endangered crafts. BUCT's closed-loop recyclability provides a perfect engineering reference for how our structural protein materials might be disassembled and reused in the future."

HUBU-WUHAN × TJUSLS_China

Why We Connect

Liquid and solid represent the two fundamental forms of pollution. HUBU guards against invisible chemical pollution in water (dissolved PAHs), while TJUSLS guards against visible solid waste on land (polyurethane plastic).

Joint Output

A shared cultural philosophy: whether applying a whole-cell catalyst for immediate on-site river screening, or engineering enzymes for industrial-scale plastic degradation, every polluted corner should be made clean again.

What We See in Each Other

HUBU-WUHAN's View:

"Rooted in the Yangtze River water culture ('tian ren he yi'), we use single living cells to accomplish both detection and degradation. TJUSLS’s genome-scale computational mining inspires us to search for more robust PAH degrading enzymes."

TJUSLS_China's View:

"We follow the Taoist teaching of 'leaving no discarded objects'. HUBU's immobilized bacterial membranes on cellulose filter discs provide a brilliant example of how complex bio-reactions can be packaged into ready-to-use kits."

USTC-iGEM × SYPHU-China

Why We Connect

One team builds a "clock," the other needs "timed drug delivery". USTC solves "how cells know what to do now" by building the infrastructure of time; SYPHU explores a live biotherapeutic product that needs "when, where, and how to release the drug".

Joint Output

A natural pair of supply and demand bridging temporal activation algorithms with localized therapeutic delivery, forming a dialogue about "time" at the molecular level.

What We See in Each Other

USTC-iGEM's View:

"Our oscillator defines time through periodic events, like traditional Shichen. SYPHU's need for a kill-switch and timed activation provides the perfect application scenario for our binary counter and effector modules."

SYPHU-China's View:

"We emphasize 'treating diseases at the source'. USTC's intracellular clock provides the precise temporal guidance our multifaceted regulatory system needs to lower mutation-driven escape risks."

LZU-CHINA × ZJUT-China

Why We Connect

Both independently selected the exact same probiotic chassis (EcN-1917) as their intestinal "sentinel," yet guard entirely different checkpoints covering the two core threats to intestinal health: acute infection vs. chronic carcinogenesis.

Joint Output

A comparative study on programming the same bacterial chassis for completely different temporal responses: immediate intervention vs. long-term risk warning.

What We See in Each Other

LZU-CHINA's View:

"We use quorum sensing to target acute infectious diarrhea. ZJUT's ability to sense faint molecular signals preceding carcinogenesis demonstrates the incredible sensitivity that can be achieved with EcN-1917."

ZJUT-China's View:

"Our G-SCOUT acts as an early warning for colorectal cancer. LZU's integrated 'sense-report-intervene' model inspires us to think about how our screening bacteria could eventually be upgraded to release therapeutics directly upon detecting danger."

Framed View Card

JLU-FBH × SUSTech-BIO

Why We Connect

Both teams work on RNA as a "messenger molecule," but stand at opposite ends of information processing. JLU-FBH encodes multiple tumor epitopes into a brand-new circRNA ("writing" from scratch), while SUSTech-BIO performs localized base editing on existing APOE4 mRNA ("revising" existing information).

Joint Output

Together they cover the two fundamental paradigms of RNA engineering: creating new messengers and revising old ones, applied to completely different disease models.

What We See in Each Other

JLU-FBH's View:

"We leverage the stability of circRNA's closed loop in the spirit of 'harmony in diversity'. SUSTech's targeted delivery design provides valuable insights for how we might direct our vaccines more precisely."

SUSTech-BIO's View:

"We edit existing genes to produce different protein versions, echoing 'changing scenery with every step'. JLU's computational pipeline for screening high MHC-I binding affinity epitopes is a masterclass in predictive structural design."
